The problem

Your firm knows more than anyone in it remembers.

Relationships live in inboxes, prospects in research lists and follow-ups in spreadsheets. On a live mandate, that split shows up in five ways.

  1. 01Duplicate outreach

    Two people email the same CFO.

    Each mandate keeps its own list, and nothing checks across them. Each email makes sense on its own. Together, they make the firm look disconnected.

    You find out from the CFO.

  2. 02The unseen warm path

    Someone at the firm already knows them.

    A partner worked with the buyer two years ago. An associate met their head of M&A at a conference. The history exists, but the person about to reach out cannot see it.

    You never find out.

  3. 03The missed follow-up

    The follow-up date passes unnoticed.

    Nobody had the schedule open. By the time someone does, the thread has been quiet for three weeks, and reopening it costs goodwill you were saving for the right moment.

    You find out three weeks late.

  4. 04The rebuilt list

    Every mandate rebuilds the same list.

    The sector map, buyer universe and contact research are rebuilt from scratch, even when the firm did the work last year.

    You pay for the same research twice.

  5. 05The handover

    An analyst leaves.

    Their contacts, their context and the reason a buyer took the last call leave with them. The firm paid for that knowledge and kept none of it.

    You find out on the next mandate.

These look like five problems. They are one: the firm does not hold its own memory.

The firm-first record

Your firm has the relationships. It just doesn’t hold them.

Relationships are built one email, meeting and mandate at a time. But most tools leave that history attached to a person or a project. When the project closes or the person moves on, the context becomes difficult to find.

Upstream keeps the company record at the centre. Each mandate adds to the same history, so the next conversation starts with what the firm already knows.

Trust and control

Nothing is captured quietly.

Each person connects their own account, chooses exclusions before any sync runs, and can see what Upstream records. By default, we use email and calendar metadata, not message bodies.

  • Permissioned per person

    Each person connects, or does not connect, their own account.

  • Headers by default

    Message bodies need a separate, explicit permission.

  • Exclusions before sync

    Domains and contacts are left out before anything is read.

  • Visible and revocable

    See what was recorded from your account, and switch it off.

FAQ

Questions partners ask.

Does Upstream read our emails?

No. By default it records headers: sender, recipients, time, subject and thread. Message bodies stay out unless a person gives separate, explicit permission.

What exactly is captured?

From each connected account: email headers and calendar meeting times and attendees. Excluded domains and contacts are set before any sync runs, and everyone can see what was recorded from their account.

Can we start with one mandate?

Yes, and we recommend it. Bring the spreadsheets for one live mandate, and the rest can follow when you are ready.

Does Upstream send emails for us?

Not on its own. An analyst can send an approach from their connected Gmail or Outlook account, or log one sent elsewhere. Upstream does not run sequences or mass email.

Is Upstream available now?

Yes, for a small number of advisory firms working with us on live mandates. What is available today, and what we are building with those firms, is listed under How it works.

More questions about trust, permissions and setup
Who can see what?

Analysts see the mandates they are on. Partners see everything. Access is enforced at the query, so a mandate you are not on never loads.

What happens when someone leaves?

Their work stays. Their contacts and history remain on the company records, credited to them, and the next person is given access to the mandate.

How is relationship strength worked out?

A meeting counts for more than an email, a reply for more than a send, and recent contact for more than old. Every score shows the reasons behind it.

Where is our data stored?

We are confirming data residency and our position under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act with counsel, and will settle both with you before any firm pays.
