The problem

Your firm already knows more than it can easily find.

Mandates live in spreadsheets, contacts in inboxes and follow-ups in someone’s calendar. On a live mandate, that split shows up in five ways.

  1. 01Duplicate outreach

    Two people email the same CFO.

    Each mandate keeps its own list, and nothing checks across them. Each email makes sense on its own. Together, they make the firm look disconnected.

    You find out from the CFO.

  2. 02The unseen history

    Someone at the firm already spoke to them.

    A colleague approached the company on last year’s mandate. The history exists, but the person about to reach out cannot see it.

    You never find out.

  3. 03The missed follow-up

    The follow-up date passes unnoticed.

    Nobody had the schedule open. By the time someone does, the thread has been quiet for three weeks, and reopening it costs goodwill you were saving for the right moment.

    You find out three weeks late.

  4. 04The rebuilt list

    Every mandate rebuilds the same list.

    The sector map, buyer universe and contact research are rebuilt from scratch, even when the firm did the work last year.

    You pay for the same research twice.

  5. 05The handover

    An analyst leaves.

    Their spreadsheets, their follow-up dates and the reason a buyer took the last call leave with them.

    You find out on the next mandate.

These look like five problems. They share one cause: the work is spread across files nobody else can see.

One shared record

Before you reach out, see what your team already knows.

Every company keeps its history across mandates. When a name you are about to approach is already on another live mandate, the company record says so, with the engagement, its status and the date of first contact.

  • Checked across live mandates.

    Companies are matched by domain, exact name and similar name, and flagged as advice, never blocked.

  • Every touch, in order.

    Emails, replies, calls and meetings are logged as a history that is added to and never rewritten.

  • The cadence does the counting.

    Follow-up dates are worked out from the first email, and stop when a company replies, bounces or declines.

Continuity

Keep the work moving, even when people move on.

Projects, contacts, follow-ups and activity stay in the firm’s workspace, not in someone’s personal files. When work changes hands, the next person opens the same record.

  • A trail of who did what.

    Each project keeps its activity: companies added, emails logged, replies recorded, tasks completed.

  • Work that is declared, not remembered.

    Tasks sit beside the cadence, attached to a project, a company or a contact, with an owner and a due date.

  • Archived before anything is deleted.

    Removing a record archives it first, so the history stays readable and can be restored.

For the person leaving, their work stays credited to them. For the person arriving, they start with context, not a blank sheet.

FAQ

Questions teams ask.

What does Upstream do today?

It runs the work around a mandate: projects and engagements, the firm’s company database, contacts, sourcing, an outreach desk with follow-up dates worked out for you, analytics and Excel import.

Does Upstream read our emails?

No. Today it can send an email from a connected Gmail or Outlook account when a person chooses to, and it records the outreach your team logs. Reading email and calendar is a future phase, and will need each person’s permission.

Can we start with one mandate?

Yes, and we recommend it. Bring the spreadsheets for one live mandate, and the rest can follow when you are ready.

Can we bring our existing spreadsheets?

Yes. Upload the client workbooks, map each sheet to a project and its engagements, and review what Upstream found before anything is written.

Who can see what?

Analysts see the mandates they are assigned to. Partners see everything. Access is checked on every list, so a mandate you are not on does not load.

More questions about setup, sending and what comes next
What happens when someone leaves?

Their projects, contacts, follow-ups and activity stay in the firm’s workspace, credited to them, and the next person is given access to the mandate.

Does Upstream send sequences or mass email?

No. Each email is written or reviewed and sent by a person, and the follow-up schedule tells them when.

What is coming next?

Relationship intelligence: memory drawn from email and calendar with each person’s permission, warm introduction paths and better recommendations. The roadmap above lists what is available now and what comes later.

Where is our data stored?

We are confirming data residency and our position under India’s Digital Personal Data Protection Act with counsel, and will settle both with you before any firm pays.
